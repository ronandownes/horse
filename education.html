RIDER EDUCATION

How we teach

Good riding is easier to remember when the rider understands the purpose of an aid, the horse's response and what to change next.

Explain → Observe → Feedback

One clear idea. One thing to notice. One useful correction.
LEG AID

Ask, then become quiet.

A clear leg aid asks the horse to respond. Once the response comes, the rider becomes quiet rather than continuing to nag.

POSITION

Balance before force.

A stable, mobile position helps the rider move with the horse rather than using the reins or gripping for security.

CONTACT

Feel, follow, guide.

Rein contact should support communication. The aim is consistent feel rather than bracing or pulling.

RHYTHM

Consistency creates confidence.

On the flat or approaching a fence, rhythm gives both horse and rider more time to organise the movement.

Progressive rider development

Horse Sport Ireland's current coaching material emphasises a progressive, systematic approach to developing horse and rider. This demo uses that idea as a design principle: explain what a rider is learning, why it matters and what normally comes next.
