A centre with a teaching identity

More than time in the saddle.

Good coaching should help a rider understand what the horse feels, what the rider is asking, and what success looks like. The aim is not simply to complete an exercise. It is to become a clearer, calmer rider.

Process before outcome. Position, balance and correct aids come first.

Learn before you book

Three ideas that change the ride.

This site should teach as well as sell. These short lessons give parents and riders a feel for the coaching philosophy before they arrive.

01 · THE LEG AID

The leg is a squeeze, not a kick.

“Squeezing invites; kicking confuses.”

A quiet lower leg asks the horse clearly. Once the horse responds, the rider becomes quiet again. Clarity comes from timing and consistency, not force.

AskFeel the responseRelease
02 · BALANCE

Hips over heels.

Your body is your anchor. Balance lets you move with the horse instead of using the reins for security.

03 · CONTACT

Soft hands allow soft horses.

Feel, follow and guide. The rein is a line of communication, not something to brace against.

COACHING METHOD

Explain → Observe → Feedback.

One clear idea, one thing to notice, one useful correction. Riders improve without being overloaded.

Showjumping

Build the ride before the fence.

Showjumping is part of the centre’s history and identity. A modern page should do more than say “we specialise in showjumping”: it can explain the progression from balance and rhythm to poles, related distances, small fences and complete rounds.

  • Rhythm first. A consistent approach gives the horse a clear, predictable ride.
  • Position before height. Riders develop balance and confidence before increasing technical difficulty.
  • Schooling and leagues. The brochure records showjumping leagues throughout the year; current dates can be published directly on the site.
  • Competition preparation. Course riding, lines, turns and decision-making can be presented as a visible learning pathway.

Since 1985

A Ryan family equestrian tradition.

The centre’s brochure records that Tipperary Equestrian Centre was established in 1985 by the Ryan family, growing naturally from a lifetime interest in owning, breeding, showing and jumping horses and ponies.

Set in the Golden Vale at Mill Road in Thurles and only a short drive from the M8, the centre has developed as a place for riders at different levels to learn, train and enjoy horses. A finished site can preserve that family story while presenting today’s services much more clearly.
